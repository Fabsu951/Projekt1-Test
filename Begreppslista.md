# Glossary – Financial Management and Investment
Lectures 1–5. T = tax rate, r = interest rate or required return, n = number of periods.

## 1. Financial statements
- **Primary financial statements**: the four main reports: income statement, balance sheet, statement of cash flows and statement of changes in equity.
- **Income statement**: shows profit or loss over a period. $\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Profit}$
- **Statement of financial position / Balance sheet**: a snapshot at a specific date. Liabilities are shown as positive amounts. $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$
- **Usefulness of the balance sheet**: shows liquidity, solvency, capital structure and risk.
- **Statement of cash flows**: actual cash in and out, split into operating, investing and financing. Profit ≠ cash flow. $\text{Opening cash} + \text{Net cash flow} = \text{Closing cash}$
- **Statement of changes in equity**: explains why equity changed.
- **Comprehensive income**: OCI (other comprehensive income) is certain unrealized value changes. $\text{Comprehensive income} = \text{Net income} + \text{OCI}$
- **Omit**: leave out.

## 2. Assets
- **Current assets**: turned into cash within 12 months. $= \text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments} + \text{AR} + \text{Inventory} + \text{Prepaid expenses}$
- **Non-current assets**: held for more than 1 year, e.g. PPE, intangibles and long-term investments.
- **Short-term or long-term investment**: current if the company intends to sell within 12 months, otherwise non-current.
- **Accounts receivable (AR)**: money customers owe the company. Current.
- **Net accounts receivable**: AR after expected bad debts. $= \text{AR} - \text{Allowance for doubtful accounts}$
- **Inventory**: raw materials, WIP and finished goods. Current. Becomes COGS when sold.
- **Inventory allowance**: writes inventory down to its net realizable value (net inventory). $\text{Net inventory} = \text{Inventory} - \text{Inventory allowance}$
- **Marketable investments**: securities that are easy to sell. Usually current.
- **T-Bill, T-Note, T-Bond**: government debt securities. Bills mature within 1 year, notes in 2–10 years and bonds in more than 10 years.
- **PPE (Property, plant & equipment)**: buildings, machinery and vehicles. Non-current, depreciated.
- **Intangible assets**: non-physical assets, e.g. patents, trademarks, licenses and goodwill.
- **Patent**: an exclusive legal right for a limited time. Intangible.
- **Goodwill**: arises only in an acquisition (M&A). $\text{Goodwill} = \text{Purchase price} - \text{Fair value of identifiable net assets}$

## 3. Liabilities
- **Current liabilities**: due within 12 months. $\text{Liabilities} = \text{Current} + \text{Non-current}$
- **Accounts payable (AP)**: money the company owes suppliers. Current.
- **Accrued expenses**: expenses incurred but not yet paid. Current.
- **Notes payable**: a formal written debt. Current if due within 12 months, otherwise non-current.
- **Interest payable**: interest incurred but not yet paid. Current.
- **Bonds payable**: debt from issued bonds. Usually non-current; the part due within 12 months is current.
- **Provision**: a liability with uncertain amount or timing. Split into current and non-current by when it will be used.
- **Warranty provision**: expected future warranty costs. $= \text{Sales} \times \text{Estimated warranty \%}$
- **Pension provision**: estimated future pension obligation. Usually non-current.

## 4. Equity and dividends
- **Equity**: $= \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} = \text{Share capital} + \text{Share premium} + \text{Retained earnings} - \text{Treasury shares}$
- **Residual claim**: the owners' claim on what remains after all liabilities are paid.
- **Shareholders' equity – ordinary**: equity belonging to ordinary (common) shareholders, excluding preferred shares.
- **Average ordinary shareholders' equity**: $= \dfrac{\text{Opening equity} + \text{Closing equity}}{2}$
- **Par value**: nominal value per share, not the market price.
- **Share capital**: $= \text{Number of shares} \times \text{Par value}$
- **Share premium**: $\text{Issue price} = \text{Par value} + \text{Share premium}$
- **Retained earnings**: $\text{Ending RE} = \text{Beginning RE} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends for the year}$
- **Treasury shares**: the company's own repurchased shares. Reduce equity; no votes and no dividends.
- **Weighted average number of shares**: used in EPS. New issues and repurchases count only for the months they were outstanding. A share dividend or split is applied back to the start of the year. $= \sum \text{Shares outstanding} \times \dfrac{\text{Months outstanding}}{12}$
- **Dividends**: $= \text{Number of shares} \times \text{Dividend per share}$
- **Cash dividend**: dividend paid in cash.
- **Share/stock dividend**: dividend paid in new shares.
- **Dividend yield**: dividend relative to the share price.
- **Preferred dividends**: paid to preferred shareholders before ordinary shareholders. Not an expense; reduce retained earnings. $= \text{Nominal value} \times \text{Dividend rate}$
- **Total payout**: what is returned to shareholders through the dividend policy and share repurchases. $= \text{Dividends} + \text{Share repurchases}$

## 5. Income and expenses
- **Revenue**: income from the core business.
- **Net sales**: $= \text{Sales revenue} - \text{Sales returns \& allowances} - \text{Sales discounts}$
- **Gain**: profit from a non-core transaction. $\text{Gain} = \text{Selling price} - \text{Book value}$
- **Expense/loss**: reduces profit. $\text{Loss} = \text{Book value} - \text{Selling price}$
- **Goods available for sale**: $= \text{Beginning inventory} + \text{Purchases}$
- **COGS (Cost of goods sold)**: $= \text{Goods available for sale} - \text{Ending inventory}$
- **Purchases**: $= \text{COGS} + \text{Ending inventory} - \text{Beginning inventory}$
- **Gross profit**: $= \text{Net sales} - \text{COGS}$
- **SG&A (Selling, general & administrative expenses)**: overhead costs, not part of production cost.
- **EBIT (Earnings before interest and taxes)**: $= \text{Net sales} - \text{COGS} - \text{SG\&A} - \text{Other operating expenses}$
- **EBITDA**: EBIT before depreciation and amortization. $= \text{EBIT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Amortization}$
- **Interest expense**: mainly comes from long-term debt. $= \text{Loan} \times \text{Interest rate}$
- **EBT (Earnings before tax)**: also called taxable income. $= \text{EBIT} - \text{Interest expense}$
- **After-tax interest expense**: $= \text{Interest expense} \times (1 - T)$
- **Interest tax shield**: interest is tax deductible, so debt lowers the tax bill. $= \text{Interest expense} \times T$
- **Bad debt expense**: estimated customer losses. The allowance for doubtful accounts reduces AR. $= \text{AR} \times \text{Estimated loss \%}$
- **Cost allocation**: spreading a cost over periods, products or departments.
- **Depreciation**: for tangible assets. Not a cash payment. $= \dfrac{\text{Cost}}{\text{Useful life}}$
- **Amortization**: for intangible assets with a limited life. $= \dfrac{\text{Cost}}{\text{Useful life}}$
- **30% rule**: Swedish declining-balance depreciation. $\text{Depreciation for the year} = \text{Remaining value} \times 30\%$

## 6. Standards and financial instruments
- **IASB (International Accounting Standards Board)**: issues IFRS. Older standards are called IAS.
- **IFRS 9**: financial instruments. Covers classification (amortized cost, FVOCI, FVTPL), impairment using ECL (Expected Credit Loss) and hedge accounting.
- **FVTPL (Fair value through profit or loss)**: value changes go directly into profit or loss. Used for trading securities.
- **Unrealized gain/loss**: value change on an asset still held. $= \text{Market value} - \text{Cost}$
- **Realized gain/loss**: gain or loss when the asset is sold. $= \text{Selling price} - \text{Cost}$

## 7. Cash flows
- **Operating activities**: day-to-day business, e.g. customers, suppliers and salaries & wages.
- **Investing activities**: buying and selling assets.
  - **Purchase fixed assets**: outflow.
  - **Investment in securities**: equity (shares) or debt (bonds). Buying is an outflow, selling is an inflow.
  - **Make a loan** (lend money to others): outflow.
- **Financing activities**: cash flows to and from owners and lenders.
  - **Share issuance / equity invested**: inflow.
  - **Borrowing / debt invested**: inflow. Borrowing can be private (bank loan) or public (bonds).
  - **Repay a loan**: outflow.
  - **Dividends paid** and **share buybacks**: outflows.
- **Interest paid**: operating or financing under IFRS; operating under US GAAP.
- **Interest and dividends received**: operating or investing under IFRS; operating under US GAAP.
- **Cash outflows**: all payments made.
- **Life cycle**: Introduction → Growth → Mature → Shakeout → Decline. Cash flow pattern (Operating / Investing / Financing):
  - **Introduction**: − / − / +
  - **Growth**: + / − / +
  - **Mature**: + / − / −
  - **Shakeout**: mixed signs
  - **Decline**: − / + / +/−

## 8. Ratios
**Ratio analysis**: ratios are grouped into profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency and market ratios. Always compare over time and with the industry.

**Liquidity** (ability to pay short-term obligations)
- **Current ratio**: above 1 is good. $= \dfrac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$
- **Acid-test (quick) ratio**: excludes inventory and prepaid expenses. $= \dfrac{\text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments} + \text{Net AR}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$
- **Cash ratio**: $= \dfrac{\text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$

**Solvency** (long-term debt risk)
- **Debt ratio**: $= \dfrac{\text{Total liabilities}}{\text{Total assets}}$
- **Times interest earned (TIE)**: how many times EBIT covers the interest. $= \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest expense}}$

**Efficiency**
- **Asset turnover**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net sales}}{\text{Average total assets}}$
- **Inventory turnover**: use net inventory. Too high or too low are both bad, as there is an optimal level. $= \dfrac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average net inventory}}$
  - **Days inventory**: $= \dfrac{365}{\text{Inventory turnover}}$
- **AR turnover**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net credit sales}}{\text{Average net AR}}$
  - **DSO (Days sales outstanding)**: $= \dfrac{365}{\text{AR turnover}}$
- **AP turnover**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net credit purchases}}{\text{Average AP}}$
  - **DPO (Days payable outstanding)**: $= \dfrac{365}{\text{AP turnover}}$

**Profitability**
- **Gross profit margin**: always higher than the net profit margin. $= \dfrac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Net sales}}$
- **Net profit margin**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Net sales}}$
- **After-tax operating income margin**: $= \dfrac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Net sales}} = \dfrac{\text{EBIT} \times (1 - T)}{\text{Net sales}}$
- **ROA (Return on assets)**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Average total assets}}$
- **ROE (Return on equity)**: compared with the cost of equity. $= \dfrac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Average ordinary shareholders' equity}}$
- **ROC / ROR (Return on capital / Rate of return)**: compared with WACC. $= \dfrac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Invested capital}}$

**Market**
- **EPS (Earnings per share)**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Weighted average number of ordinary shares}}$
- **P/E (Price/earnings ratio)**: $= \dfrac{\text{Market price per share}}{\text{EPS}}$
- **Dividend payout ratio**: $= \dfrac{\text{Dividends}}{\text{Net income}} = \dfrac{\text{DPS}}{\text{EPS}}$
- **Total payout ratio**: $= \dfrac{\text{Dividends} + \text{Share repurchases}}{\text{Net income}}$
- **Retention ratio**: $= 1 - \text{Payout ratio}$

## 9. DuPont
Core idea: Return = Profitability × Efficiency (× Leverage).

**2-factor**

$$\text{ROA} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}}$$

**3-factor**

$$\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}$$

(Profit margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

**4-factor**

$$\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}$$

(Tax burden × Pre-tax margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

**5-factor**

$$\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}$$

(Tax burden × Debt burden × EBIT margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

**The factors**
- **Tax burden**: $= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}}$
- **Debt (interest) burden**: close to 1 means low interest burden. $= \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}}$
- **EBIT margin (operating margin)**: $= \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Sales}}$
- **Pre-tax margin**: used in the 4-factor model. $= \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{Sales}}$
- **Asset turnover**: $= \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}}$
- **Equity multiplier (leverage)**: $= \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}$

## 10. Cost of capital, EVA and valuation
- **Cost of capital**: the return required by owners (cost of equity) and lenders (cost of debt).
- **Cost of assets**: the required return on the business's assets. Roughly equal to WACC.
- **Total capitalization**: $= \text{Debt} + \text{Equity}$
  - WACC weights: $\dfrac{E}{D+E} \text{ and } \dfrac{D}{D+E}$
- **WACC (Weighted average cost of capital)**: $= \dfrac{E}{D+E} \times R_e + \dfrac{D}{D+E} \times R_d \times (1 - T)$
- **NOPAT (Net operating profit after tax)**: $= \text{EBIT} \times (1 - T) = \text{Net income} + \text{Interest expense} \times (1 - T)$
- **Capital charge**: $= \text{Invested capital} \times \text{WACC}$
- **EVA (Economic value added)**: positive EVA means value is created. $= \text{NOPAT} - \text{Invested capital} \times \text{WACC} = (\text{ROC} - \text{WACC}) \times \text{Invested capital}$
- **MVA (Market value added)**: market value of equity minus book value of equity. $= \text{MV} - \text{BV}$
- **Abnormal (residual) earnings**: profit above what the owners require. $= \text{Net income}_t - r_e \times \text{BVE}_{t-1}$
- **Residual income valuation**: $\text{MV} = \text{BVE} + \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \dfrac{\text{Abnormal earnings}_t}{(1 + r_e)^t}$
- **FMCG (Fast-moving consumer goods)**: everyday goods sold quickly at low margins, e.g. Procter & Gamble's personal care, household items, beverages and OTC drugs.

## 11. Time value of money
- **Time value of money (TVM)**: money today is worth more than the same amount later, because it can earn interest.
- **Discount rate**: the rate r used to discount future cash flows, e.g. the required return or WACC. Higher r gives lower PV.
- **Discounting / compounding**: discounting moves money back in time (FV → PV); compounding moves it forward (PV → FV).

**Single amounts**
- **Future value (FV)**: $\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r)^n$
- **Present value (PV)**: $\text{PV} = \dfrac{\text{FV}}{(1 + r)^n} = \text{FV} \times \text{DF}$
- **Discount factor (DF)**: today's value of 1 kr received in n periods. $\text{DF} = \dfrac{1}{(1 + r)^n}$
- **Compound factor**: what 1 kr today grows to in n periods. $= (1 + r)^n$
- **Simple interest**: interest only on the original amount. $\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r \times n)$
- **Compound interest**: interest on interest. $\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r)^n$

**Compounding frequency (m = periods per year)**
- **FV with m periods per year**: $\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{m \times n}$
- **PV with m periods per year**: $\text{PV} = \dfrac{\text{FV}}{\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{m \times n}}$
- **Effective annual rate (EAR)**: $\text{EAR} = \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^m - 1$
- **Continuous compounding, FV**: $\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times e^{r \times n}$
- **Continuous compounding, PV**: $\text{PV} = \text{FV} \times e^{-r \times n}$
- **EAR with continuous compounding**: $\text{EAR} = e^r - 1$
- **Real interest rate (Fisher)**: $(1 + r_{\text{nominal}}) = (1 + r_{\text{real}}) \times (1 + \text{inflation})$

**Multiple cash flows**
- **PV of uneven cash flows**: $\text{PV} = \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \dfrac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t}$
- **FV of uneven cash flows**: $\text{FV} = \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \text{CF}_t \times (1 + r)^{n-t}$
- **NPV (Net present value)**: invest if NPV > 0. $\text{NPV} = -\text{CF}_0 + \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \dfrac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t}$

**Annuities (equal payment C every period for n periods)**
- **PV of an annuity**: $\text{PV} = C \times \dfrac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r}$
- **Annuity factor**: PV of 1 kr per period. $\text{AF} = \dfrac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r}$
- **FV of an annuity**: $\text{FV} = C \times \dfrac{(1 + r)^n - 1}{r}$
- **Annuity due**: payments at the start of each period, so multiply by (1 + r). $\text{PV}_{\text{due}} = \text{PV}_{\text{annuity}} \times (1 + r)$
- **FV of an annuity due**: $\text{FV}_{\text{due}} = \text{FV}_{\text{annuity}} \times (1 + r)$
- **Loan / annuity payment**: the equal payment that repays a loan. $C = \dfrac{\text{PV} \times r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}$
- **PV of a growing annuity**: grows at rate g. $\text{PV} = \dfrac{C_1}{r - g} \times \left[1 - \left(\dfrac{1 + g}{1 + r}\right)^n\right]$
- **FV of a growing annuity**: $\text{FV} = C_1 \times \dfrac{(1 + r)^n - (1 + g)^n}{r - g}$

**Perpetuities (cash flows forever)**
- **Perpetuity**: first payment in one period. $\text{PV} = \dfrac{C}{r}$
- **Perpetuity due**: first payment today. $\text{PV} = C + \dfrac{C}{r}$
- **Delayed perpetuity**: first payment at time t. $\text{PV}_0 = \dfrac{C}{r} \times \dfrac{1}{(1 + r)^{t-1}}$
- **Growing perpetuity**: requires r > g. Used in the Gordon growth model and terminal value. $\text{PV} = \dfrac{C_1}{r - g}$

**Solving for r and n**
- **Interest rate**: $r = \left(\dfrac{\text{FV}}{\text{PV}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1$
- **Number of periods**: $n = \dfrac{\ln\left(\dfrac{\text{FV}}{\text{PV}}\right)}{\ln(1 + r)}$
- **Doubling time (exact)**: $n = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln(1 + r)}$
- **Rule of 72**: approximate years to double, with r in percent. $\text{Years to double} \approx \dfrac{72}{r}$
