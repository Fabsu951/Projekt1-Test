1Financial statements

  • Primary financial statements: the four main reports: income statement, balance sheet, statement of cash flows and statement of changes in equity.
  • Income statement: shows profit or loss over a period. Revenue−Expenses=Profit\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Profit}
  • Statement of financial position / Balance sheet: a snapshot at a specific date. Liabilities are shown as positive amounts. Assets=Liabilities+Equity\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}
  • Usefulness of the balance sheet: shows liquidity, solvency, capital structure and risk.
  • Statement of cash flows: actual cash in and out, split into operating, investing and financing. Profit ≠ cash flow. Opening cash+Net cash flow=Closing cash\text{Opening cash} + \text{Net cash flow} = \text{Closing cash}
  • Statement of changes in equity: explains why equity changed.
  • Comprehensive income: OCI (other comprehensive income) is certain unrealized value changes. Comprehensive income=Net income+OCI\text{Comprehensive income} = \text{Net income} + \text{OCI}
  • Omit: leave out.

2Assets

  • Current assets: turned into cash within 12 months. =Cash+Short-term investments+AR+Inventory+Prepaid expenses= \text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments} + \text{AR} + \text{Inventory} + \text{Prepaid expenses}
  • Non-current assets: held for more than 1 year, e.g. PPE, intangibles and long-term investments.
  • Short-term or long-term investment: current if the company intends to sell within 12 months, otherwise non-current.
  • Accounts receivable (AR): money customers owe the company. Current.
  • Net accounts receivable: AR after expected bad debts. =AR−Allowance for doubtful accounts= \text{AR} - \text{Allowance for doubtful accounts}
  • Inventory: raw materials, WIP and finished goods. Current. Becomes COGS when sold.
  • Inventory allowance: writes inventory down to its net realizable value (net inventory). Net inventory=Inventory−Inventory allowance\text{Net inventory} = \text{Inventory} - \text{Inventory allowance}
  • Marketable investments: securities that are easy to sell. Usually current.
  • T-Bill, T-Note, T-Bond: government debt securities. Bills mature within 1 year, notes in 2–10 years and bonds in more than 10 years.
  • PPE (Property, plant & equipment): buildings, machinery and vehicles. Non-current, depreciated.
  • Intangible assets: non-physical assets, e.g. patents, trademarks, licenses and goodwill.
  • Patent: an exclusive legal right for a limited time. Intangible.
  • Goodwill: arises only in an acquisition (M&A). Goodwill=Purchase price−Fair value of identifiable net assets\text{Goodwill} = \text{Purchase price} - \text{Fair value of identifiable net assets}

3Liabilities

  • Current liabilities: due within 12 months. Liabilities=Current+Non-current\text{Liabilities} = \text{Current} + \text{Non-current}
  • Accounts payable (AP): money the company owes suppliers. Current.
  • Accrued expenses: expenses incurred but not yet paid. Current.
  • Notes payable: a formal written debt. Current if due within 12 months, otherwise non-current.
  • Interest payable: interest incurred but not yet paid. Current.
  • Bonds payable: debt from issued bonds. Usually non-current; the part due within 12 months is current.
  • Provision: a liability with uncertain amount or timing. Split into current and non-current by when it will be used.
  • Warranty provision: expected future warranty costs. =Sales×Estimated warranty %= \text{Sales} \times \text{Estimated warranty \%}
  • Pension provision: estimated future pension obligation. Usually non-current.

4Equity and dividends

  • Equity: =Assets−Liabilities=Share capital+Share premium+Retained earnings−Treasury shares= \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} = \text{Share capital} + \text{Share premium} + \text{Retained earnings} - \text{Treasury shares}
  • Residual claim: the owners' claim on what remains after all liabilities are paid.
  • Shareholders' equity – ordinary: equity belonging to ordinary (common) shareholders, excluding preferred shares.
  • Average ordinary shareholders' equity: =Opening equity+Closing equity2= \dfrac{\text{Opening equity} + \text{Closing equity}}{2}
  • Par value: nominal value per share, not the market price.
  • Share capital: =Number of shares×Par value= \text{Number of shares} \times \text{Par value}
  • Share premium: Issue price=Par value+Share premium\text{Issue price} = \text{Par value} + \text{Share premium}
  • Retained earnings: Ending RE=Beginning RE+Net income−Dividends for the year\text{Ending RE} = \text{Beginning RE} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends for the year}
  • Treasury shares: the company's own repurchased shares. Reduce equity; no votes and no dividends.
  • Weighted average number of shares: used in EPS. New issues and repurchases count only for the months they were outstanding. A share dividend or split is applied back to the start of the year. =∑Shares outstanding×Months outstanding12= \sum \text{Shares outstanding} \times \dfrac{\text{Months outstanding}}{12}
  • Dividends: =Number of shares×Dividend per share= \text{Number of shares} \times \text{Dividend per share}
  • Cash dividend: dividend paid in cash.
  • Share/stock dividend: dividend paid in new shares.
  • Dividend yield: dividend relative to the share price.
  • Preferred dividends: paid to preferred shareholders before ordinary shareholders. Not an expense; reduce retained earnings. =Nominal value×Dividend rate= \text{Nominal value} \times \text{Dividend rate}
  • Total payout: what is returned to shareholders through the dividend policy and share repurchases. =Dividends+Share repurchases= \text{Dividends} + \text{Share repurchases}

5Income and expenses

  • Revenue: income from the core business.
  • Net sales: =Sales revenue−Sales returns & allowances−Sales discounts= \text{Sales revenue} - \text{Sales returns \& allowances} - \text{Sales discounts}
  • Gain: profit from a non-core transaction. Gain=Selling price−Book value\text{Gain} = \text{Selling price} - \text{Book value}
  • Expense/loss: reduces profit. Loss=Book value−Selling price\text{Loss} = \text{Book value} - \text{Selling price}
  • Goods available for sale: =Beginning inventory+Purchases= \text{Beginning inventory} + \text{Purchases}
  • COGS (Cost of goods sold): =Goods available for sale−Ending inventory= \text{Goods available for sale} - \text{Ending inventory}
  • Purchases: =COGS+Ending inventory−Beginning inventory= \text{COGS} + \text{Ending inventory} - \text{Beginning inventory}
  • Gross profit: =Net sales−COGS= \text{Net sales} - \text{COGS}
  • SG&A (Selling, general & administrative expenses): overhead costs, not part of production cost.
  • EBIT (Earnings before interest and taxes): =Net sales−COGS−SG&A−Other operating expenses= \text{Net sales} - \text{COGS} - \text{SG\&A} - \text{Other operating expenses}
  • EBITDA: EBIT before depreciation and amortization. =EBIT+Depreciation+Amortization= \text{EBIT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Amortization}
  • Interest expense: mainly comes from long-term debt. =Loan×Interest rate= \text{Loan} \times \text{Interest rate}
  • EBT (Earnings before tax): also called taxable income. =EBIT−Interest expense= \text{EBIT} - \text{Interest expense}
  • After-tax interest expense: =Interest expense×(1−T)= \text{Interest expense} \times (1 - T)
  • Interest tax shield: interest is tax deductible, so debt lowers the tax bill. =Interest expense×T= \text{Interest expense} \times T
  • Bad debt expense: estimated customer losses. The allowance for doubtful accounts reduces AR. =AR×Estimated loss %= \text{AR} \times \text{Estimated loss \%}
  • Cost allocation: spreading a cost over periods, products or departments.
  • Depreciation: for tangible assets. Not a cash payment. =CostUseful life= \dfrac{\text{Cost}}{\text{Useful life}}
  • Amortization: for intangible assets with a limited life. =CostUseful life= \dfrac{\text{Cost}}{\text{Useful life}}
  • 30% rule: Swedish declining-balance depreciation. Depreciation for the year=Remaining value×30%\text{Depreciation for the year} = \text{Remaining value} \times 30\%

6Standards and financial instruments

  • IASB (International Accounting Standards Board): issues IFRS. Older standards are called IAS.
  • IFRS 9: financial instruments. Covers classification (amortized cost, FVOCI, FVTPL), impairment using ECL (Expected Credit Loss) and hedge accounting.
  • FVTPL (Fair value through profit or loss): value changes go directly into profit or loss. Used for trading securities.
  • Unrealized gain/loss: value change on an asset still held. =Market value−Cost= \text{Market value} - \text{Cost}
  • Realized gain/loss: gain or loss when the asset is sold. =Selling price−Cost= \text{Selling price} - \text{Cost}

7Cash flows

  • Operating activities: day-to-day business, e.g. customers, suppliers and salaries & wages.
  • Investing activities: buying and selling assets.
    • Purchase fixed assets: outflow.
    • Investment in securities: equity (shares) or debt (bonds). Buying is an outflow, selling is an inflow.
    • Make a loan (lend money to others): outflow.
  • Financing activities: cash flows to and from owners and lenders.
    • Share issuance / equity invested: inflow.
    • Borrowing / debt invested: inflow. Borrowing can be private (bank loan) or public (bonds).
    • Repay a loan: outflow.
    • Dividends paid and share buybacks: outflows.
  • Interest paid: operating or financing under IFRS; operating under US GAAP.
  • Interest and dividends received: operating or investing under IFRS; operating under US GAAP.
  • Cash outflows: all payments made.
  • Life cycle: Introduction → Growth → Mature → Shakeout → Decline. Cash flow pattern (Operating / Investing / Financing):
    • Introduction: − / − / +
    • Growth: + / − / +
    • Mature: + / − / −
    • Shakeout: mixed signs
    • Decline: − / + / +/−

8Ratios

Ratio analysis: ratios are grouped into profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency and market ratios. Always compare over time and with the industry.

Liquidity (ability to pay short-term obligations)

  • Current ratio: above 1 is good. =Current assetsCurrent liabilities= \dfrac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}
  • Acid-test (quick) ratio: excludes inventory and prepaid expenses. =Cash+Short-term investments+Net ARCurrent liabilities= \dfrac{\text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments} + \text{Net AR}}{\text{Current liabilities}}
  • Cash ratio: =Cash+Short-term investmentsCurrent liabilities= \dfrac{\text{Cash} + \text{Short-term investments}}{\text{Current liabilities}}

Solvency (long-term debt risk)

  • Debt ratio: =Total liabilitiesTotal assets= \dfrac{\text{Total liabilities}}{\text{Total assets}}
  • Times interest earned (TIE): how many times EBIT covers the interest. =EBITInterest expense= \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest expense}}

Efficiency

  • Asset turnover: =Net salesAverage total assets= \dfrac{\text{Net sales}}{\text{Average total assets}}
  • Inventory turnover: use net inventory. Too high or too low are both bad, as there is an optimal level. =COGSAverage net inventory= \dfrac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average net inventory}}
    • Days inventory: =365Inventory turnover= \dfrac{365}{\text{Inventory turnover}}
  • AR turnover: =Net credit salesAverage net AR= \dfrac{\text{Net credit sales}}{\text{Average net AR}}
    • DSO (Days sales outstanding): =365AR turnover= \dfrac{365}{\text{AR turnover}}
  • AP turnover: =Net credit purchasesAverage AP= \dfrac{\text{Net credit purchases}}{\text{Average AP}}
    • DPO (Days payable outstanding): =365AP turnover= \dfrac{365}{\text{AP turnover}}

Profitability

  • Gross profit margin: always higher than the net profit margin. =Gross profitNet sales= \dfrac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Net sales}}
  • Net profit margin: =Net incomeNet sales= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Net sales}}
  • After-tax operating income margin: =NOPATNet sales=EBIT×(1−T)Net sales= \dfrac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Net sales}} = \dfrac{\text{EBIT} \times (1 - T)}{\text{Net sales}}
  • ROA (Return on assets): =Net incomeAverage total assets= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Average total assets}}
  • ROE (Return on equity): compared with the cost of equity. =Net income−Preferred dividendsAverage ordinary shareholders’ equity= \dfrac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Average ordinary shareholders' equity}}
  • ROC / ROR (Return on capital / Rate of return): compared with WACC. =NOPATInvested capital= \dfrac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Invested capital}}

Market

  • EPS (Earnings per share): =Net income−Preferred dividendsWeighted average number of ordinary shares= \dfrac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Weighted average number of ordinary shares}}
  • P/E (Price/earnings ratio): =Market price per shareEPS= \dfrac{\text{Market price per share}}{\text{EPS}}
  • Dividend payout ratio: =DividendsNet income=DPSEPS= \dfrac{\text{Dividends}}{\text{Net income}} = \dfrac{\text{DPS}}{\text{EPS}}
  • Total payout ratio: =Dividends+Share repurchasesNet income= \dfrac{\text{Dividends} + \text{Share repurchases}}{\text{Net income}}
  • Retention ratio: =1−Payout ratio= 1 - \text{Payout ratio}

9DuPont

Core idea: Return = Profitability × Efficiency (× Leverage).

2-factor

ROA=Net incomeSales×SalesAssets\text{ROA} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}}

3-factor

ROE=Net incomeSales×SalesAssets×AssetsEquity\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}

(Profit margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

4-factor

ROE=Net incomeEBT×EBTSales×SalesAssets×AssetsEquity\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}

(Tax burden × Pre-tax margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

5-factor

ROE=Net incomeEBT×EBTEBIT×EBITSales×SalesAssets×AssetsEquity\text{ROE} = \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}} \times \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Sales}} \times \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \times \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}

(Tax burden × Debt burden × EBIT margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier)

The factors

  • Tax burden: =Net incomeEBT= \dfrac{\text{Net income}}{\text{EBT}}
  • Debt (interest) burden: close to 1 means low interest burden. =EBTEBIT= \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}}
  • EBIT margin (operating margin): =EBITSales= \dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Sales}}
  • Pre-tax margin: used in the 4-factor model. =EBTSales= \dfrac{\text{EBT}}{\text{Sales}}
  • Asset turnover: =SalesAssets= \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}}
  • Equity multiplier (leverage): =AssetsEquity= \dfrac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}

10Cost of capital, EVA and valuation

  • Cost of capital: the return required by owners (cost of equity) and lenders (cost of debt).
  • Cost of assets: the required return on the business's assets. Roughly equal to WACC.
  • Total capitalization: =Debt+Equity= \text{Debt} + \text{Equity}
    • WACC weights: ED+E and DD+E\dfrac{E}{D+E} \text{ and } \dfrac{D}{D+E}
  • WACC (Weighted average cost of capital): =ED+E×Re+DD+E×Rd×(1−T)= \dfrac{E}{D+E} \times R_e + \dfrac{D}{D+E} \times R_d \times (1 - T)
  • NOPAT (Net operating profit after tax): =EBIT×(1−T)=Net income+Interest expense×(1−T)= \text{EBIT} \times (1 - T) = \text{Net income} + \text{Interest expense} \times (1 - T)
  • Capital charge: =Invested capital×WACC= \text{Invested capital} \times \text{WACC}
  • EVA (Economic value added): positive EVA means value is created. =NOPAT−Invested capital×WACC=(ROC−WACC)×Invested capital= \text{NOPAT} - \text{Invested capital} \times \text{WACC} = (\text{ROC} - \text{WACC}) \times \text{Invested capital}
  • MVA (Market value added): market value of equity minus book value of equity. =MV−BV= \text{MV} - \text{BV}
  • Abnormal (residual) earnings: profit above what the owners require. =Net incomet−re×BVEt−1= \text{Net income}_t - r_e \times \text{BVE}_{t-1}
  • Residual income valuation: MV=BVE+∑t=1∞Abnormal earningst(1+re)t\text{MV} = \text{BVE} + \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \dfrac{\text{Abnormal earnings}_t}{(1 + r_e)^t}
  • FMCG (Fast-moving consumer goods): everyday goods sold quickly at low margins, e.g. Procter & Gamble's personal care, household items, beverages and OTC drugs.

11Time value of money

  • Time value of money (TVM): money today is worth more than the same amount later, because it can earn interest.
  • Discount rate: the rate r used to discount future cash flows, e.g. the required return or WACC. Higher r gives lower PV.
  • Discounting / compounding: discounting moves money back in time (FV → PV); compounding moves it forward (PV → FV).

Single amounts

  • Future value (FV): FV=PV×(1+r)n\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r)^n
  • Present value (PV): PV=FV(1+r)n=FV×DF\text{PV} = \dfrac{\text{FV}}{(1 + r)^n} = \text{FV} \times \text{DF}
  • Discount factor (DF): today's value of 1 kr received in n periods. DF=1(1+r)n\text{DF} = \dfrac{1}{(1 + r)^n}
  • Compound factor: what 1 kr today grows to in n periods. =(1+r)n= (1 + r)^n
  • Simple interest: interest only on the original amount. FV=PV×(1+r×n)\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r \times n)
  • Compound interest: interest on interest. FV=PV×(1+r)n\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r)^n

Compounding frequency (m = periods per year)

  • FV with m periods per year: FV=PV×(1+rm)m×n\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{m \times n}
  • PV with m periods per year: PV=FV(1+rm)m×n\text{PV} = \dfrac{\text{FV}}{\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{m \times n}}
  • Effective annual rate (EAR): EAR=(1+rm)m−1\text{EAR} = \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^m - 1
  • Continuous compounding, FV: FV=PV×er×n\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times e^{r \times n}
  • Continuous compounding, PV: PV=FV×e−r×n\text{PV} = \text{FV} \times e^{-r \times n}
  • EAR with continuous compounding: EAR=er−1\text{EAR} = e^r - 1
  • Real interest rate (Fisher): (1+rnominal)=(1+rreal)×(1+inflation)(1 + r_{\text{nominal}}) = (1 + r_{\text{real}}) \times (1 + \text{inflation})

Multiple cash flows

  • PV of uneven cash flows: PV=∑t=1nCFt(1+r)t\text{PV} = \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \dfrac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t}
  • FV of uneven cash flows: FV=∑t=1nCFt×(1+r)n−t\text{FV} = \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \text{CF}_t \times (1 + r)^{n-t}
  • NPV (Net present value): invest if NPV > 0. NPV=−CF0+∑t=1nCFt(1+r)t\text{NPV} = -\text{CF}_0 + \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \dfrac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t}

Annuities (equal payment C every period for n periods)

  • Ordinary annuity: payments at the end of each period. PV=C×1−(1+r)−nr=C×PVAF\text{PV} = C \times \dfrac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r} = C \times \text{PVAF}
  • PVAF (Present value annuity factor): PV of 1 kr per period; the sum of the discount factors. PVAF=1−(1+r)−nr=DF1+DF2+⋯+DFn\text{PVAF} = \dfrac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r} = \text{DF}_1 + \text{DF}_2 + \dots + \text{DF}_n
  • FV of an annuity: FV=C×(1+r)n−1r=C×FVAF\text{FV} = C \times \dfrac{(1 + r)^n - 1}{r} = C \times \text{FVAF}
  • FVAF (Future value annuity factor): FVAF=(1+r)n−1r\text{FVAF} = \dfrac{(1 + r)^n - 1}{r}
  • Annuity = difference of two perpetuities: one starting now minus one starting after n periods. PV=Cr−Cr×1(1+r)n\text{PV} = \dfrac{C}{r} - \dfrac{C}{r} \times \dfrac{1}{(1 + r)^n}
  • Annuity due: payments at the start of each period (rent, leasing, "starting today"). Always worth more. PVdue=C×PVAF×(1+r)=C+C×PVAFn−1\text{PV}_{\text{due}} = C \times \text{PVAF} \times (1 + r) = C + C \times \text{PVAF}_{n-1}
  • FV of an annuity due: FVdue=C×FVAF×(1+r)\text{FV}_{\text{due}} = C \times \text{FVAF} \times (1 + r)
  • Loan / annuity payment: the equal payment that repays a loan. C=PVPVAF=PV×r1−(1+r)−nC = \dfrac{\text{PV}}{\text{PVAF}} = \dfrac{\text{PV} \times r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}
  • Loan payment, annuity due: C=PVPVAF×(1+r)C = \dfrac{\text{PV}}{\text{PVAF} \times (1 + r)}
  • Savings needed per period: C=FVFVAFC = \dfrac{\text{FV}}{\text{FVAF}}
  • PV of a growing annuity: grows at rate g. PV=C1r−g×[1−(1+g1+r)n]\text{PV} = \dfrac{C_1}{r - g} \times \left[1 - \left(\dfrac{1 + g}{1 + r}\right)^n\right]
  • FV of a growing annuity: FV=C1×(1+r)n−(1+g)nr−g\text{FV} = C_1 \times \dfrac{(1 + r)^n - (1 + g)^n}{r - g}
  • Monthly payments: use the monthly rate and number of months. rmonth=r12,nmonths=12×nr_{\text{month}} = \dfrac{r}{12}, \quad n_{\text{months}} = 12 \times n

Retirement planning (two annuities)

  • Step 1, amount needed at retirement: PV of the withdrawals X over the retirement years. Needed=X×PVAF\text{Needed} = X \times \text{PVAF}
  • Step 2, saving per year: the savings must grow to the amount needed. C=NeededFVAFC = \dfrac{\text{Needed}}{\text{FVAF}}
  • Lump sum today instead: PV0=Needed(1+r)n\text{PV}_0 = \dfrac{\text{Needed}}{(1 + r)^n}
  • Withdrawals forever: use a perpetuity. Needed=Xr\text{Needed} = \dfrac{X}{r}

Perpetuities (cash flows forever)

  • Perpetuity: first payment in one period. PV=Cr\text{PV} = \dfrac{C}{r}
  • Perpetuity due: first payment today. PV=C+Cr=Cr×(1+r)\text{PV} = C + \dfrac{C}{r} = \dfrac{C}{r} \times (1 + r)
  • Delayed perpetuity: first payment at time t. PV0=Cr×1(1+r)t−1\text{PV}_0 = \dfrac{C}{r} \times \dfrac{1}{(1 + r)^{t-1}}
  • Growing perpetuity: requires r > g. Used in the Gordon growth model and terminal value. PV=C1r−g\text{PV} = \dfrac{C_1}{r - g}
  • Growing perpetuity due: first payment C today, then growing at g. PV=C+C×(1+g)r−g=C×(1+r)r−g\text{PV} = C + \dfrac{C \times (1 + g)}{r - g} = \dfrac{C \times (1 + r)}{r - g}

Solving for r and n

  • Interest rate: r=(FVPV)1n−1r = \left(\dfrac{\text{FV}}{\text{PV}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1
  • Number of periods: n=ln⁡(FVPV)ln⁡(1+r)n = \dfrac{\ln\left(\dfrac{\text{FV}}{\text{PV}}\right)}{\ln(1 + r)}
  • Doubling time (exact): n=ln⁡2ln⁡(1+r)n = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln(1 + r)}
  • Rule of 72: approximate years to double, with r in percent. Years to double≈72r\text{Years to double} \approx \dfrac{72}{r}
