### Question 1 — Soft vs rigid robots (10 pts)
(a) Give **three** advantages of soft robots over rigid-bodied robots, and **two** remaining challenges of soft robots. (5)
(b) Explain why industrial robot arms are usually fenced off from human workers. (2)
(c) An electric-motor-driven arm has a much lower payload-to-self-weight ratio than a human arm. Give the physical reason, using
the density of the motor's materials. (3)

### Question 2 — Joint torque and muscle strength (10 pts)
The biceps brachii inserts on the radius 4 cm from the elbow's centre of rotation, at an angle of 70° to the bone. The muscle tension is 500 N.
(a) Calculate the torque produced at the elbow. (4)
(b) Calculate the force component parallel to the bone. Does it produce torque? Describe its effect on the joint when the elbow is flexed to less
than 90°. (3)
(c) The muscle has a physiological cross-sectional area of 6 cm². What is the maximum tension it can develop? (3)

### Question 3 — Fibre architecture (10 pts)
Two muscles have the same volume V₀. Muscle P is parallel-fibred with fibre length 12 cm. Muscle Q is unipennate with fibre length 3 cm and
pennation angle 25°. Both have the same specific fibre tension.
(a) Find the ratio of tendon force F_Q / F_P. (5)
(b) Which muscle can shorten more and faster? Which is cheaper (ATP per unit force)? Explain briefly. (3)
(c) Where in the leg would you expect each type, and why? (2)

### Question 4 — Muscle physiology (10 pts)
(a) During a concentric contraction, what happens to the A band, the I band and the H zone? Why? (3)
(b) What is the role of ATP in the cross-bridge cycle? (2)
(c) Explain the force-length relationship of a sarcomere: why is force low both when it is very short and when it is very stretched? (3)
(d) Distinguish *wave summation* and *motor-unit recruitment*. (2)

### Question 5 — Hydrostats (12 pts)
(a) An earthworm segment is modelled as a thin-walled cylinder with radius r = 3 mm and wall thickness t = 0.3 mm, at internal pressure p = 1 kPa.
Derive and calculate the longitudinal and circumferential wall stresses. (6)
(b) A squid tentacle stalk (muscular hydrostat) decreases its diameter by 15 %. By what percentage does it lengthen? Compare with the
linearized formula dL/L = −2 dD/D and explain the difference. (4)
(c) State the assumption that makes (b) valid. (2)

### Question 6 — McKibben artificial muscle (12 pts)
A McKibben muscle has braid fibres of length b = 0.30 m, each wound n = 2 turns around the tube.
(a) Starting from $L = b\cos\theta$ and $n\pi D = b\sin\theta$, derive the axial force $F(P,\theta)$. (5)
(b) Find the critical braid angle at which the force vanishes. (2)
(c) At P = 300 kPa and θ = 30°, calculate L, D and F. (3)
(d) What is the maximum contraction ratio (relative to the length at θ = 30°)? (2)

### Question 7 — Levers (10 pts)
A person holds a 5 kg mass in the hand with the forearm horizontal. The biceps inserts 4 cm from the elbow and pulls vertically. The hand load acts 35 cm
from the elbow. Ignore the forearm's weight.
(a) Which lever class is this? Compute the mechanical advantage and the distance advantage. (4)
(b) What biceps force is needed? (3)
(c) If the biceps shortens at 0.10 m/s, how fast does the hand move? What principle relates (b) and (c)? (3)

### Question 8 — Walking (12 pts)
(a) Derive the maximum walking speed of a person with leg length L, modelling walking as an inverted pendulum. (5)
(b) Calculate it for L = 0.8 m on Earth, on Mars (g = 3.71 m/s²) and on the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²). (3)
(c) Explain why racewalkers can exceed this speed. (2)
(d) Give the duty-factor criterion that separates walking from running, and the duty factor of a slowly walking tortoise. (2)

### Question 9 — Jumping (8 pts)
A 70 kg person crouches and then extends, raising the centre of mass by s = 0.40 m before take-off. The average ground reaction force during the extension is
N = 1600 N.
(a) Calculate the jump height after take-off and the take-off speed. (4)
(b) Assuming constant acceleration, how long does the push-off last? (2)
(c) Why does a countermovement jump reach higher than a squat jump from rest? (2)

### Question 10 — Swimming (6 pts)
(a) A fish of tissue density 1080 kg/m³ lives in sea water (1026 kg/m³). What fraction of its body volume must its gas-filled swim bladder occupy for
neutral buoyancy? Neglect the gas density. (2)
(b) Compare the Reynolds number of a 1 m tuna swimming at 3 m/s (ρ = 1026 kg/m³, μ = 1.0×10⁻³ Pa·s) with that of a 2 µm bacterium at 10 µm/s.
Which kind of drag dominates for each? (2)
(c) An eel's body wave travels backward at 1.25 times its swimming speed. What is its Froude efficiency? (2)
