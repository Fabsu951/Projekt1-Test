# Three Hard Problems — weeks 1–5

Each problem combines several topics in one chain. Use only the formula sheet. g = 9.81 m/s².
Aim for about 25 minutes per problem. Solutions: [hard-problems-solutions.md](hard-problems-solutions.md).

## Problem 1 — Standing on tiptoe: lever + pennate muscle (20 pts)
A 70 kg person rises onto the toes of **one** foot. Model the foot as a rigid lever pivoting about the ball of the foot.
Body weight acts down through the ankle, **12 cm** from the ball of the foot. The Achilles tendon pulls straight up on the heel,
**17 cm** from the ball of the foot.

(a) Which lever class is this? Calculate the mechanical advantage MA and the distance advantage DA. (3)

(b) Calculate the Achilles tendon force needed to hold the position. (3)

(c) The calf muscle is pennate with a fibre angle of 20°. What total **fibre** tension is needed, and what minimum physiological
cross-sectional area (PCSA) must the muscle have? (4)

(d) Compare two muscles of the **same volume, 60 cm³**: muscle P (pennate, fibre length 4 cm, angle 20°) and muscle Q
(parallel, fibre length 16 cm). Calculate the maximum tendon force of each. Which one can hold the person on tiptoe? (6)

(e) The calf muscle shortens at 2 cm/s. How fast does the body rise? Show that power is conserved. (4)

## Problem 2 — McKibben elbow (20 pts)
A robotic elbow is driven by a McKibben muscle with braid fibre length **b = 0.40 m**, **n = 3** turns, starting braid angle
**θ₀ = 25°**, and supply pressure **P = 400 kPa**.

(a) Calculate the starting length L₀ and diameter D₀. (3)

(b) Calculate the pulling force at the start. (4)

(c) How far can the muscle shorten at most? Give the stroke in cm and as a percentage of L₀. (4)

(d) The muscle attaches 5 cm from the elbow joint and the load hangs 30 cm from the joint. With the forearm horizontal and the
muscle pulling perpendicular to it, what is the heaviest mass the elbow can **start** to lift? (4)

(e) Halfway through the motion the braid angle is θ = 45°. What mass can it hold there at the same pressure? What pressure
would be needed to hold the mass from (d) at θ = 45°? Explain why the force drops as the muscle contracts. (5)

## Problem 3 — Basketball player: jump and sprint (20 pts)
A **75 kg** basketball player.

(a) In a countermovement jump he lowers his centre of mass by **s = 0.45 m**, then pushes off with an average ground force of
**N = 1900 N**. Calculate the acceleration during push-off, the take-off speed, the push-off time and the jump height. (6)

(b) He wants to jump **0.80 m** with the same crouch depth. What average ground force does he need? Give it in newtons and in
body weights. (3)

(c) If he keeps N = 1900 N instead, how deep must he crouch to jump 0.80 m? (2)

(d) Sprinting: each leg has a mass of **9 kg**, the horizontal propulsive force is **F = 450 N**, and the stride length is
**L = 1.1 m**. Using the stride-by-stride energy model, find v₁ after the first stride, the top speed, and the number of strides
needed to reach 90 % of top speed. (6)

(e) Explain briefly why the countermovement helps the jump, and why the top sprint speed depends on the **leg** mass rather than the
**body** mass. (3)
